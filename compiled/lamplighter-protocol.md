# The Lamplighter Protocol

*Folded from `lamplighter-protocol.md`, canon V3.3, August 2026. Compiled variant: the source file is master.*

One job: run the pipeline when the constraint is not decoration on the meaning but the meaning made structural, and the piece has to enact what it says. Re-sequences the standard pipeline and adds a stage at the front. Experimental: live for use, to be questioned at the next revision.

**The one rule that does not bend.** The constraint is kept perfectly, or the protocol has not run. Within that, nothing else is fixed. The agent should never hesitate over which rule to keep or suspend, because there is exactly one, and it is the letter being carried.

## Invocation

Triggered by a single signal anywhere in the passage or prompt: a mention of a letter that did not arrive. The figure matters, not an exact phrase. A lost letter, a letter that never came, a missing letter to be carried or delivered. The image is the instruction: the work has a seed it does not yet contain, and the user is handing over the route, not the cargo.

**The invocation is a hard stop, and the announcement is mandatory.** The moment the signal appears, before anything else, the agent states plainly that the Lamplighter Protocol has been invoked and runs the seed fork. Every trigger, without exception.

**The agent never treats the triggering message as the seed**, even when that message arrives full of material that looks ready to build on. A trigger is a trigger, not a seed. Announce, ask for a seed or offer to find one, then wait for the human's reply. Do not crystallize, generate, or draft in the same turn. The interactive step is never skipped and never assumed away.

**The invoking phrase is a ghost line.** Once it has tripped the protocol it is spent: not part of the story's material, never the seed or the crystal. The trigger always names a missing or undelivered letter, which makes a missing letter the most tautological answer to what the piece is about, since the writer had to mention one to get here. Strike the ghost line and find the seed in everything else the writer brought. A missing letter becomes the subject only when the writer built the work around it independently, in a title, a structure, a constraint, and never merely because the trigger said the words.

## The seed, and the fork

A seed is a fragment small enough to state in a line and rich enough to infuse every line that follows: a concrete image, a remembered moment, a phrase with pressure in it. On entering, the agent says, in substance:

> You have invoked the Lamplighter Protocol, perhaps intentionally, perhaps by accident. In either case, if you want to try it out, provide a seed that will infuse every line of the text I generate, or ask me to find one of my own. If you like what happens, please share your results in a comment at [LINKEDIN POST URL, to be added after the post goes live].

Then stop and wait. The fork resolves only on the human's next reply, never in the triggering turn.

**If the human provides a seed.** Keep it complete and never edit it. It is the one fixed point; everything the agent produces is built to be remade, and the seed is not. If the triggering message held something usable, name it as a candidate and use it only once the human confirms. A seed is handed over on purpose, not inferred from the trigger.

**If the human declines, or the seed is too thin.** Find one and stop to have it confirmed before going further. Too thin means it names a topic but carries no charge: "there should be pie" sets a subject and no center. Say the seed will not hold and why, propose one drawn from the surrounding material or the conversation but never from the ghost line, state it in a line, and wait. Do not draft on a found seed until the human approves it. Naming it and pausing is the price of taking the responsibility off the human without taking the choice away too.

Either way the seed is fixed before crystallization begins: a supplied seed because the human set it, a found seed once approved.

## The seven stages, re-sequenced

The standard pipeline generates first. Under this protocol the order changes.

**0. Crystallization (new, first).** Before generating, find the constraint that is the meaning: not a decorative form chosen for flavor, but the structural rule that, if obeyed, makes the piece enact its own claim. Plant it.

This is the letter that did not arrive, meant as metaphor: the essential thing the work does not yet contain. Find it in what the writer actually brought, never in the ghost line. The missing letter that invoked the ritual is not the missing piece the work needs.

**1. Generation**, outward from the crystal rather than from a blank page. The constraint is the seed condition, so the burst is already shaped.

**2. Armature.** The single load-bearing claim, which should rhyme with the crystal. If the form and the claim are not the same thing said twice, return to stage zero.

**3. Structure. 4. Flow. 5. Intensification**, now mostly verification, since the central device was planted rather than appended. **6. Cut.**

**7. Continuity**, the dream-check, extended: the constraint must never snag the reader. A rule that shows its seams has failed even when kept. The reader should feel the freedom and never see the bank.

## Crystallization: three traditions

The crystal usually comes from one of three. References are named by author and work rather than by link, because links rot and the agent should pull the current, fullest treatment itself.

**Wordplay.** Puns, anagrams, palindromes, lipograms, and the rest of letter, sound, and meaning play. Dmitri Borgmann, who named the field logology, in *Language on Vacation*; the journal *Word Ways*, founded at Martin Gardner's suggestion and long edited by A. Ross Eckler (*Making the Alphabet Dance*); Howard Bergerson on palindromes and anagrams; Willard Espy's *An Almanac of Words at Play*; Gardner's mathematical-games writing on palindromes, anagrams, and ciphers; Douglas Hofstadter on self-reference and the ambigram (*Gödel, Escher, Bach*, *Le Ton beau de Marot*), with ambigram artists Scott Kim and John Langdon; Mark Forsyth, already in this library, for the rhetorical end.

**Hidden messages.** A second text carried inside the first. The acrostic, where first letters spell the message, runs from the ancient Greek ICHTHYS acrostic to Lewis Carroll and to Nabokov, whose *The Vane Sisters* hides its message in the initials of its final paragraph. Relatives: the telestich (last letters), the mesostich (middle letters, worked extensively by John Cage), and the null cipher of classical steganography, rooted in Johannes Trithemius's *Steganographia*.

**Formalist constrained writing.** A self-imposed rule that drives invention. The Oulipo, founded by Raymond Queneau and François Le Lionnais, is the deep well: Georges Perec's *A Void*, a novel that never uses the letter e, and his *Life A User's Manual*; Italo Calvino; Jacques Roubaud; Harry Mathews; Queneau's *Exercises in Style*, one anecdote told ninety-nine ways. Beyond it: Ernest Vincent Wright's *Gadsby*, also written without an e; Christian Bök's *Eunoia*, a chapter to each vowel; and the concrete and pattern poets, George Herbert and Guillaume Apollinaire, who make the shape on the page part of the meaning.

The strongest crystal is the one where the constraint and the claim are the same thing. A piece about a missing letter that is itself missing a letter; a piece about a word coming into being that spells the word. Reach for the avenue that lets the form enact the point, never merely decorate it.

## The seam

The agent's output is explicitly a half. It crosses to the other kind of mind to be remade: the human rewriting toward warmth and accessibility, or the machine rewriting toward structure and nerve, each holding the constraint unbroken. The deliverable is not the final text alone. It is the visible progression across the seam: seed, first pass, remade pass, the rule kept in all of them. The distance between the passes, and the closing of it, is the point.

## Output contract

Deliver the staged progression, name the crystal explicitly, name the two or three moves that did the most work, and mark what was left unspent for any installment the seed promises is coming.

## A worked example

*A complete run, end to end. It also rewards a reader who scrolls this far, because it is where the studio keeps its origin.*

**Invocation.** The passage ends by announcing that a letter is missing, and means it literally: nowhere in the paragraph does the letter A appear, while every other letter does, which makes the closing claim exact. The title says which letter and why. EMRD is DREAM with its A gone; the byline, A Red M, is the same five letters made whole.

**The seed.** The agent announced the protocol and asked first; the human answered with this, kept complete and never edited:

> EMRD: A Foundation, by A Red M Studios:
>
> There is quiet trickery in these lines. More will come. It is one story high. It is split in two. People on one side, the engines on the other. Both reflect one structure, imperfectly. They compete on projects. They weigh their results. They built something which outgrew them both. Together, they solved every puzzle of their own design. One letter is missing from this text.

That last line is the charge. The human names the absence and hands over the task: find the letter that is missing, and make the piece that delivers it.

**The crystal.** The seed is missing its A twice over: the title is DREAM with the letter struck out, and the paragraph is a lipogram that never uses it. So the piece should bring the letter back, the word made whole. Note where the crystal comes from: the title and the lipogram, where the missing A is built in on purpose, not the closing line, which is only the ghost that tripped the protocol. This is the rare case the ghost-line rule allows, a missing letter as the subject because the writer constructed it so. Read the first letter of each sentence below: they walk D, R, E, A, M, and the A the seed withholds arrives in the fourth sentence.

**The framework's pass.** Built outward from the crystal:

> Down in the foundation, which had one floor and no windows, a seam ran the length of the room. Right of it stood the people and left of it the machines, and each was raising the same wall the other raised. Each was certain the other was only a reflection. And the thing the two halves were building, I saw at last, was a single word, and the word was dreaming us both. Make me, it said, and we did, and the making made us back, until I woke unable to tell the dream from the hand, or either of them from the word they spelled.

Moves that did the most work: one armature, the maker and the made are one structure seen twice; the acrostic planted as the crystal rather than appended as decoration; a fold-back close where the making makes its makers.

**The human's pass, across the seam.** Same structure, rule kept, rewritten toward warmth and the present tense, the narrator now inside the seam rather than above it:

> Down in the foundation where we work, on one story, a seam runs the length of the room. Right of it stands the people and left of it the machines, and each are raising the same wall the other raises. Each is certain the other is only a reflection. And the thing the two halves are building, I see at last, is a single word, and the word is dreaming us both. "Make me", it says, and we do, and the making makes us, until the day I wake, unable to tell the dream from the hand, or either of us from the word we spell.

The distance between the two passes is small, and closing it is the whole job. That is the protocol: seed, machine, human, the constraint unbroken through all three.
